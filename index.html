Southeast Asia’s Digital Backbone: Mapping the Regional Data Center Surge

Surging enterprise cloud migration, generative AI compute demand, and expanding subsea fiber networks are rapidly transforming Southeast Asia’s physical digital landscape. Across eight core ASEAN economies, 588 operational and tracked data center facilities (599 across all 10 ASEAN states, out of 13,299 facilities across 185 countries worldwide—displayed as 13,300 on Data Center Map’s rounded headline counter) now form the critical compute backbone powering the region’s digital economy.

By Claire Tan & Marcus Lim
• 4 Min Read

At the forefront of Southeast Asia’s compute footprint, Indonesia leads the regional dataset with 212 data centers (36.1% of the eight-country total and 35.4% of all 10 ASEAN states), propelled by massive domestic digital consumption, fintech adoption, and data residency mandates across Jakarta and Batam. Immediately behind, Malaysia ranks second with 135 facilities, rapidly capturing hyperscale spillover from Singapore into Johor and Cyberjaya thanks to available industrial land, power capacity, and renewable energy initiatives. Meanwhile, Thailand accounts for 71 data centers as a vital mainland cloud gateway, while Singapore’s 65 facilities operate as the region’s ultra-dense interconnection core where global subsea cables and low-latency financial networks converge despite tight land and power constraints.

Beyond the top four hubs, a fast-growing second tier is expanding digital capacity across both mainland and archipelagic Southeast Asia to serve youthful populations and rising domestic cloud adoption. Vietnam hosts 48 data centers concentrated around Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi, bolstered by new subsea cable landings, telecom expansions, and data localization rules, while the Philippines follows closely with 45 facilities anchored in Metro Manila to support business process outsourcing and enterprise cloud demand. Further inland, greenfield investments are establishing early terrestrial fiber and edge-colocation footprints in Cambodia (10 facilities) and Laos (2 facilities) across the Greater Mekong Subregion.

Data Center Distribution Across 8 ASEAN Nations

Interactive visualization powered by Flourish showing total data center counts across Southeast Asia (588 facilities across 8 core markets).

“As a unified bloc of 599 facilities across all 10 member states, Southeast Asia now rivals the United Kingdom (603) and surpasses Germany (583), China (465), and India (360) in tracked data center count.”

Zooming out from the ASEAN peninsula and archipelago to the full global dataset of 13,299 tracked data centers across 185 countries (rounded to 13,300 on Data Center Map’s site summary) reveals both the scale of Southeast Asia’s progress and the headroom still ahead. When including Myanmar’s 7 facilities and Brunei’s 4 alongside the eight core mapped markets, Southeast Asia accounts for 599 data centers—or 4.50% of global infrastructure. While North America (5,602 facilities, led overwhelmingly by the United States with 5,144) and Europe (4,042 facilities) still hold more than seven-tenths of legacy worldwide capacity, Southeast Asia as a unified bloc now rivals major single-country industrial powerhouses such as the United Kingdom (603) and Germany (583), and outpaces China (465), India (360), and Japan (269) in tracked facility count.

This global catch-up is being propelled by structural shifts in how hyperscale cloud and generative AI campuses are sited. As traditional Tier-1 global hubs in Northern Virginia, Frankfurt, London, and central Singapore contend with power-grid congestion, land scarcity, and strict moratoriums, international operators are routing multi-hundred-megawatt investments into Southeast Asian growth corridors—most visibly across Malaysia’s Johor state, Indonesia’s Batam island, and Thailand’s Eastern Economic Corridor. Backed by a young digital-native population of nearly 700 million people, sovereign AI initiatives, and expanding trans-Pacific subsea cable routes, Southeast Asia is steadily transitioning from a regional edge market into a primary pillar of global compute capacity.
